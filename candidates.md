# Candidates — Six Words, One Story

Each candidate is exactly six words. Judged against the brief: a whole story
first, then a turn or a whole life implied, every word earning its place,
clean read-aloud rhythm, and something left unsaid for the reader to fill in.

1. **Wife planted roses; rocket returned empty.**
   Setup and loss in one breath; the garden keeps growing for someone who
   never comes home. Strong, but the space-loss beat is a familiar one.

2. **Finally sober, he apologized. Too late.**
   A whole life in six words — addiction, recovery, regret. The turn lands
   hard on the last two words. Slightly well-worn shape, though.

3. **Dog waited daily; owner never returned.**
   Loyalty against absence. Moving, but it tells the reader exactly what
   happened and leaves little to fill in.

4. **Separated at birth; reunited buying milk.**
   The comic entry: a lifetime's separation closed in a supermarket aisle.
   Fun turn, but the laugh outweighs the story.

5. **She sold the ring. Bought freedom.**
   Clean arc, clean turn. Risks reading as a slogan rather than a story.

6. **Memorized her number; she changed it.**
   Small, true, quietly sad. The turn is gentle — maybe too gentle.

7. **Dad's last voicemail: "Call me back."**
   A death, a message, and a regret the reader has to assemble themselves:
   the call was never returned while returning it still mattered. "Last"
   does the turning; the quoted message gives the dead man his own voice;
   nothing is spelled out. Every word earns its place and it reads cleanly
   aloud.

## Pick

**#7 — Dad's last voicemail: "Call me back."**

It is the only candidate that is a complete story *and* withholds its most
important fact (the unreturned call) for the reader to supply. Drafted into
main.ts, then tightened through revision before finalizing.
